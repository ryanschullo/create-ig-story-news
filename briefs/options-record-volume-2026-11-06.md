## Verified report and preserved scope

- The article reports **more than 112 million U.S. options contracts** traded
  during the **Thursday, November 5** stock-market rout, the highest volume on
  record. The graphic represents the threshold as **MORE THAN / 112M**, not an
  exact 112,000,000 count.
- The report says volume was **53% above October levels**. Although its prose
  uses “average daily volumes,” it does not give a precise October baseline or
  calculation method. Preserve the reported comparison without inventing an
  exact October average, derived baseline, comparison bar or dollar figure.
- **Just over half** the contracts were puts. The graphic may shorten this to
  “over half”; no exact percentage, put/call ratio or put contract count is
  invented from that qualitative description.
- These are **contract trading volumes**, not dollar notional, dollars of
  premium, open interest, net new positions or a count of traders.
- Put volume includes buying and selling and cannot by itself establish that
  all the trades are newly bearish positions. No claim of aggregate net
  bearish exposure or exclusively institutional/retail activity is made.
- The source refers only to **clearing data**; it does not name a provider or
  supply a detailed product/venue breakdown. Do not silently assign the
  record to OCC, Cboe alone, a broker platform or equity-only options.
- The article's VIX commentary and index moves are omitted to keep the graphic
  focused on options activity. This is not a broad market-close graphic.

The source calls this a record. The historical record series was not
independently reconstructed: attempted ordinary access to relevant OCC pages
and Investing.com/Bloomberg counterparts returned 403. No access restriction
