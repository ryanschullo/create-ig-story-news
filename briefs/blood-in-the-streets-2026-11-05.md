## Crucial scope and record distinctions

The Reuters report explicitly assigns the $2.5T loss to the **Nasdaq-100**, not the Nasdaq Composite. The dollar-loss card and daily-results row therefore name different indices deliberately. The Composite's 11.9% decline from its record supports the correction narrative; the graphic uses its separate −5.5% daily result.

MarketWatch's publicly accessible deck calls the S&P500's estimated $2.9T market-cap loss its largest one-day dollar loss on record. This is a publisher-reported nominal-dollar record, **not an all-time percentage-loss record** or an independently reconstructed historical series. The graphic attributes the record to MarketWatch. No sufficiently explicit source established a Nasdaq-100 historical market-cap-loss record; that stronger requested assertion is omitted. NY Post's phrase “Nasdaq companies” is too imprecise to establish the Nasdaq-100 record.

The indices share constituents. The $2.9T and $2.5T are reported estimates for overlapping universes; they are **not added into $5.4T of unique U.S. stock-market losses**. Market-cap declines are not equivalent to cash leaving the market or realized losses for every investor. The “Blood in the Streets” headline is the user's financial metaphor.

The context sentence condenses reporting about high valuations, uncertain returns on enormous AI spending and possible post-election data-center restrictions. It is not a measured new return-on-capital series or proof that a single factor caused every decline. No proposed nationwide construction moratorium is described as enacted.
