# Instagram Story graphics

For creating or revising news graphics, read and follow
`.agents/skills/ig-story-news/SKILL.md`.

The user's marked content area is a required layout constraint. Fit every part of
the news content inside it, including the full headline, image, body text, chart
axes and legend, and source attribution. Never satisfy the constraint by clipping
content or making text too small to read.
The latest enlarged bounds are x=80–985 and y=140–1560 on a 1080 × 1920 canvas,
scaled proportionally, with at least 24 px of inner padding. Use the enlarged
space for readable content rather than retaining the older, tighter margins.

Always use Lato Black (weight 900) for headlines and labels, and Georgia for
body text, captions, and source attribution. Follow the skill's full font
assignments; do not silently substitute other fonts.

At 1080 × 1920, use a hard minimum of 30 px for all newly authored text,
including dates, chart labels, captions, qualifiers, and sources. Headlines
remain at least 48 px and body copy at least 32 px. Scale proportionally for
other canvas sizes. The 30 px floor supersedes the drinking graphic's former
22–24 px small text and exceeds the user's requested four-point increase.
Reflow or shorten content instead of shrinking below these minimums; check
actual computed font sizes and final-image legibility. Preserve original
source-chart artwork and enlarge the whole chart when its labels are too small.
Apply this to new graphics and requested revisions.

For new graphics and requested revisions, omit the footer
`Background: editorial illustration` and equivalent background-production
credits. Keep news-source attribution, dates, and necessary factual qualifiers.
Record background provenance in briefs and asset documentation. Leave existing
exports unchanged unless the user requests a revision.

Use `MONEYBERG NEWS BRIEF` as the recurring brand label for new graphics and
requested revisions. Keep all three words on one compact line in Lato Black
(weight 900), secondary to the story headline, around 30–34 px at 1080 px canvas
width. Adjust spacing and size to fit rather than wrapping. Keep it inside the
content bounds. Leave historical exports unchanged unless requested.

Use these exact names when assigning topics or displaying category labels.
For daily article requests, search across this list and prioritize fresh,
relevant reporting within the requested total. The skill defines each topic's
scope. Retain relevant coverage of the U.S. economy, business, finance, layoffs,
Kevin Warsh, Trump's economic actions, Iran, and Russia–Ukraine within the
applicable topics. The user welcomes useful suggestions for additional topics;
identify suggestions clearly and expand the saved list as the user adds topics.

Assign nuclear-power-focused stories primarily to `NUCLEAR ENERGY INVESTING`,
counting each story once. Follow the skill's nuclear scope, project-milestone,
capacity, financial, and subject-specific design guidance.

For `OPTIONS INVESTING`, actively seek multiple fresh candidates and include
strong options stories when available. Keep its category badge separate from
the compact `MONEYBERG NEWS BRIEF` brand label. Follow the skill's options
sourcing, metric, original-chart, and ink/plum/indigo-and-teal design guidance.

Daily U.S. market wrap graphics must show the Nasdaq Composite, S&P 500, and
Dow Jones Industrial Average results for the same trading date. Verify each
daily percentage change and closing level; add point changes only when verified.
Never substitute Nasdaq-100 silently, invent missing data, or derive point
changes from rounded values. Label nonfinal snapshots as intraday. This rule
applies to broad market wraps, not every unrelated news graphic.

Give each subject a distinct design, palette, layout, and relevant background
imagery. Oil stories use a petroleum/charcoal/amber industrial treatment; other
subjects need their own visual approach. Power and infrastructure stories use
charcoal, steel blue, restrained electric yellow, and relevant grid imagery.
Always use the user's original uploaded gold Moneyberg coin as the only approved
coin artwork. Preserve its exact portrait, rim lettering, and circular
proportions; do not redraw it or use generated variants as substitutes.
Integrate the coin into the scene through compatible lighting, tone, surface
texture, and appropriate shadow or reflection. Compose the background around
it so it feels embedded in the environment; a detached badge or simply reducing
the opacity of a coin placed over the scene does not satisfy this rule.
Keep the entire coin, including its rim, within the top three-quarters of the
canvas: y=0–1440 on a 1080 × 1920 graphic, scaled proportionally. Reserve space
for it during composition and protect news text, charts, and source legibility.
Subject-specific imagery remains primary. This supersedes earlier lower-corner
or lower-background placement guidance for future graphics and requested
revisions; historical exports stay unchanged unless requested.
